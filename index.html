Neuroscience

How Neural Signaling Works

Every thought, memory, and sensation emerges from billions of electrochemical pulses racing through the most complex structure in the known universe.

🧬

The Neuron

Neurons are specialized cells with a soma (cell body), branching dendrites that receive incoming signals, and a long axon that transmits electrical impulses at speeds up to 120 m/s.

⚡

Action Potentials

When inputs exceed a threshold, a rapid all-or-nothing depolarization wave—an action potential—fires along the axon. This spike is the fundamental unit of neural communication.

🔗

Synaptic Transmission

At each synapse, electrical signals convert to chemical messages. Neurotransmitters cross the synaptic cleft and bind to receptors, propagating the signal onward through the network.

🌐

Connectomes

Neurons form dense, dynamic networks. The human brain's ~86 billion neurons create roughly 100 trillion synaptic connections — more than the stars in 1,000 Milky Way galaxies.

🔄

Neuroplasticity

The brain continuously rewires itself. Synaptic connections strengthen through repeated activation (LTP) and weaken through disuse — the cellular basis of all learning and memory.

🌊

Neural Oscillations

Neurons fire in coordinated rhythmic patterns — theta, alpha, beta, and gamma waves. These synchronized oscillations bind distant regions and are thought to underlie consciousness itself.
